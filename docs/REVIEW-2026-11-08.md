### 2.1 Q1 and Q5: a superblock mined before the beacon cannot attest

ChatGPT's attack (both questions): "a commitment value becomes known before the burn's Ethereum beacon; a sibling
carries the same `accepted_id_merkle_root` as the eventual B; the attacker builds a hash-only header carrying it and
mines a real superblock on top; `attest_steps` accepts it." Step 1 cannot happen. Every value in `chain` contains
the beacon, through hashes only:

    beacon hash  ⊂ payload  ⊂ tx body  →  tx_id (inclusion_with: `tx_id(&b.tx)`, blake3 "TransactionV1Id")
      →  activity leaf  →  activity_digest  →  lane_tip  →  lanes_root (SMT)  →  activity_root
      →  state_root_c  →  seq_commit_c  →  (chain_state_roots)  →  seq_commit_final == B.aimr      (run_with step 2)
    B.aimr = c_0;  c_i = H_seq(c_{i-1}, root_i)                                                   (step 1c)
    first-parent header: aimr = c_i  ⊂ header bytes  →  its hash                                   (step 1b, Stage::Hash)
    superblock: that hash  ⊂ parents field  ⊂ header bytes  →  PoW hash                           (step 3, attest_steps)

So a commitment in `chain`, a twin of it, and any hash-only header carrying either, were all computed after the
Ethereum block whose hash the burn names; and a superblock whose first-parent path reaches one was mined after
that header existed. Nothing in this depends on the header being a real chain block, which is why the brief said
membership is by commitment equality: the equality is with a value that embeds the beacon. The brief should have
spelled this chain out; it said only "a commitment that did not exist before B", and the reviewer took B's time as
unestablished.

ChatGPT's "second weakness" (`attest_roots` are prover-supplied, not authenticated) is true and harmless: a prover
can extend the chain with any roots, but every `c_i` is a hash descendant of `c_0`, so the only thing the freedom
buys is making his own post-beacon superblocks attest without a real chain block as first parent. The model already
grants the forger that (all of his superblocks count for him); what it denies him is honest superblocks mined before
the beacon, and those cannot reach `chain` (a hit would be a preimage of a real commitment). Honest superblocks
mined after B attest through real chain blocks as before (98.1% within three hops).

The 11-02 answer to "hash-only y_headers make attestation free" (REVIEW-2026-11-02 section 2.4) covers the rest:
the counted block is the one with the PoW, and its first-parent hash is in its mined bytes.

### 2.2 Q2 and Q9: the fallback is as described; kept

ChatGPT is right about the mechanics: `_remember` keeps a day's hardest reading in its slot but overwrites
`lastWork` with every settlement, so after seven days without a settlement the reference is the newest reading,
which can be lower than the week's hardest. That is what 2.11.4 says ("if the week is empty, the newest reading
rules"). Two things decide whether it matters.

A forger cannot inject a low reading. To settle a proof whose `r_bits` is his, his superblocks must be the majority
of the set the median is taken over. Over the expanded set that was cheap (F15); over attesting superblocks (the
fix) it needs more than the honest network's post-beacon production, so hashrate above 98%. Readings in the memory
are therefore honest readings of the real difficulty, consensus-valid `bits` on consensus-valid blocks.

The residual is honest: a reading taken in a dip, then a silence longer than SLOW_SPAN, then the reference is the
dip. Measured dip: 263 against 352 PH/s, 25% low for four hours; a forger would then need 37.5% of the hashrate for
the window instead of 50%. The daily maintenance burn (2.11.8) keeps the window populated, and D4 (2.13.4) makes
forgery lose money at any alpha while KAS is above $0.016. ChatGPT's recommendation, a permanent high-water mark,
would reintroduce the stall the design removed: a reference that cannot fall freezes exits after any lasting
hashrate decline (11-02's "fixed reference" was rejected for that). The alternative "fallback = hardest of the eight
slots regardless of age" is safer against the dip case and worse for liveness after a decline; neither matters
while the maintenance burn runs. Kept as is; stated in the README.

### 2.3 Q3: the question was misframed; the batch rule is right

ChatGPT: an older burn batched with a fresh one gets the fresh tau, the time term shrinks, and "there is no general
guarantee" the batch requirement covers what the old burn alone would have needed. True arithmetic, wrong model.
`tau` is not a waiting period a burn must serve; it is the forger's maximum head start. Every attester in the proof
commits (2.1) to the freshest beacon, so no attester was mined before it, so the forger's private branch cannot
have started before it, so his production is bounded by `h × r × tau_fresh`; `alpha × r × tau_fresh` is exactly
the bar that says "more than half the network for that window". An older beacon in the same batch is a weaker
bound on the same branch and adds nothing; the old burn carries no requirement of its own because the thing being
secured is B's acceptance of all of them, not each burn's age. The brief asked "strictly more attestation for the
older burn", which presumes the wrong model; DeepSeek answered inside it (and got the direction backwards: a shorter
tau is a lower bar, not "stricter") and still reached the right conclusion.

Pairing: a forger who forks B can include an honest stale burn in his fake B; that releases honest funds to the
honest recipient at the forger's expense. No gain. A burn whose beacon has aged out of EIP-2935 settling on a later
burn's beacon is the designed rescue (2.11.3), not a weakness: the later beacon bounds the branch at least as well.

### 2.4 Q4: the median over the expanded set can be stuffed (F15)

ChatGPT's order statistics are right: with a fraction f of the set the median lands at the honest
`(1 - 2f) / (2 (1 - f))` quantile at worst (f 0.4: 16.7%; f 0.45: 9.1%; f 0.49: 2%). Inside the honest ladder that
is nothing: honest blocks are consensus-valid, so their `bits` is the DAA's and the honest spread over 1.5 h is the
DAA's drift, a few percent. A forger cannot plant a stamped `bits` into the real DAG (consensus rejects it), so the
shift within the honest spread needs real hashrate f and buys a few percent. DeepSeek's "he cannot make his targets
lower than the honest targets without mining harder" is wrong (a level-30 hash is under any target down to 64x the
current difficulty; `bits` is free up to the hash), but the honest ladder is safe for the consensus reason, not
DeepSeek's.

ChatGPT's second point is the one that counts: "the median is over `expanded`, not an independent measurement; the
claimed relationship between the malicious fraction of that set and the attacker's fraction of hashrate needs
proving." It does not hold. `expanded` is every PoW-verified header the walk reaches with `daa > B.daa` and level
>= L. The guest checks PoW and parsing (`Stage::Full`), not consensus: a forger's private header may carry any
`daa_score`, any `bits` the hash allows, any parents. So he can premine level-30 superblocks at leisure, stamp
`daa` far above any future B, stamp `bits` at up to 64x, and list them as level-30 parents of his T beside the
honest ladder. They are expanded and counted (not attesting: that needs the beacon), and they move the median once
they outnumber the honest expanded set. The premined blocks never earn rewards, so each costs ~125 KAS of forgone
emission, but the forger needs only the count, not a hashrate share, and he can take weeks to accumulate it.

What he can do with it: raise the week's reference for everyone (a stall), bounded by his own proof having to pass
the bar it sets: `honest attesting (0.981 r (tau - 15 min)) + his >= alpha k r tau`, so k <= about 1.5 at the
floor and 1.9 at six hours. Computed at 9.125/min, 125 KAS per superblock, KAS $0.032:

| window | honest expanded to outnumber | k reachable | cost of the stuffing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 65 min (the floor) | ~456 | 1.5x | ~57k KAS, ~$1,800 |
| 2 h | ~958 | 1.7x | ~120k KAS, ~$3,800 |
| 6 h | ~3,150 | 1.9x | ~394k KAS, ~$12,600 |

For $1,800 a week every exit takes 1.5x longer for seven days; a $5k exit 6 h 50 instead of 4 h 35. No theft, no
lowering (a low reading is absorbed by `max(history, own)` and the fallback needs a 7-day silence), but a cheap and
repeatable griefing lever, and the statement in the brief ("the median moves only when more than half of the
expanded superblocks are the forger's, which is more than half of the hashrate over the window") is false in its
second half.

**Fix (sprint 2b): take the median over the attesting superblocks, not the expanded set.** An attester was mined
after the beacon (2.1), so it cannot be premined; the forger's share of attesters over the window is
`h / (0.981 + h)`, and moving the median needs more than half, so h > 98%. His own-ladder proof (no honest
attesters) can carry any reading but must then meet `alpha k r tau` with his own post-beacon blocks: k <= 2h, no
inflation below 50% hashrate, and a low reading is absorbed. After the fix the stamped `daa` and parents of a
private header affect nothing the relay reads (`count` is informational), which also answers ChatGPT's F11
(2.11). One-line change in `run_with` step 3 (collect attesters; `median_bits` over them) plus an adversarial test
that premines stamped superblocks beside the honest ladder and asserts the reading does not move.

### 2.6 Q6: the fee cannot block a release; the width is not a bug

Both reviewers agree, with the right mechanism: `release` debits `outstanding` by `p`, accrues `fee` from the
recipient's part, pays `toRecipient - fee`, and restores all three if the recipient refuses; `claimFees` is a
separate call that fails closed. `feeOwed` is a claim on tokens the vault holds and has already stopped owing.
`uint64` holds 18.4 trillion dollars of base units; at the factory maximum refill ($25k a day) and 10 bps the
accumulator reaches it in about two billion years. `outstanding` has the same width and USDC's whole supply is a
thousand times below it. Not a finding.

The refused tip (ChatGPT's F12) is real as accounting: when `_pay(submitter, toTip)` fails the recipient was paid,
`outstanding` was debited by `p` (tip included), and `toTip` stays in the vault counted nowhere. The tip is
best-effort by design (the submitter chose to prove; a blacklisted prover is his own problem), but the vault should
not strand USDC: **F18**, accrue a refused tip to `feeOwed`, so that `balance == outstanding + feeOwed` is an
invariant the tests can assert.

### 2.7 Q7: the close

Agreed by both: only `closeDeposits(preimage)` sets the flag, only `deposit` and `room` read it, `release`, the
bucket and `claim` do not. A stolen preimage shrinks the bridge and nothing else. Threshold holders: section 5.

### 2.8 Q8: griefing is closed; the tip can be front-run (F16)

Pinning an honest burn as skipped or already recorded needs a journal that carries that burn with other fields; the
guest recomputes `tx_id` from the body, reads the amount from the burn cells, the recipient and tip from the
payload, and the relay trusts the journal only after `VERIFIER.verify`. Closed, as both reviewers say.

ChatGPT's addition is right: `_record` stores `submitter: msg.sender` and the tip follows it. A proof in the public
mempool can be copied and submitted first; the recipient is paid correctly and the copier takes the tip. The
robot can submit through a private mempool, but "anyone can run a prover" is a trust statement and the tip is
the incentive behind it, so the proof should name its prover. **F16, sprint 2b:** the witness carries the prover's
Ethereum address, the journal carries it (20 bytes), the relay pays the tip to the journal's prover, not to
`msg.sender`. Journal becomes `KZO6`, 310 + 68 n bytes (prover at 289, n_burns at 309, burns at 310).

### 2.9 Q9: the slots

Both agree the arithmetic is sound: `fromDay = (t - SLOW_SPAN) / 1 day`, the current day and the seven before it,
eight slots, the slot a new day overwrites is eight days old and outside the window; a reading from early in the
oldest day lasts slightly longer than seven exact days (conservative). Ethereum's `block.timestamp` is the clock, a
Kaspa header cannot move it. The rest is 2.2.

### 2.10 Q10: head of line is the point; the key is the page's job

The consecutive nonce is the F5 fix; its cost is exactly ChatGPT's head-of-line dependency: deposit 102 waits for
101. A deposit "the RPC cannot serve" is a transient failure of one node: the record is in the vault's storage on
Ethereum and any archive node serves its proof at the finalized block; the guest builds status 1 from the proven
blacklist flag for a blacklisted depositor and status 0 otherwise, and the vault's own checks (`amount > 0`,
`version 0 or 8`, `payload != 0`) make every recorded deposit well-formed for the covenant. No permanent stall.

ChatGPT's F15 (a version-0 payload that is not a valid x-only key mints a cell nobody can spend): true, and the
same as sending to a wrong address. The vault takes raw script bytes; the page builds them from a `kaspa:` address
whose bech32 checksum it verifies, so a typo does not reach the vault. An on-chain curve check would catch half of
random garbage and none of a valid key the user does not hold. Not a contract matter; the README states that the
deposit's Kaspa address is final.

### 2.11 ChatGPT's F11: header fields are prover-controlled

True: `verify_header::<K>(hb, Stage::Full)` checks parsing and PoW, so `daa_score`, `bits` and the parents of a
forger's private header are his choice within what his hash allows; the guest checks no consensus rule. The
consequence is confined to the expanded set (counting and the median) and is F15; after the fix, nothing the relay
uses depends on a non-attesting header, and attesting is bound by the commitment chain, not by `daa`. Honest headers
are consensus-valid, so their fields are true. `tin.daa > bin.daa` and `count` remain informational.

### 2.12 The rebuttal round (ChatGPT, fresh chat, the verdicts file attached; same afternoon)

Asked to name the first link of 2.1's hash chain it could not verify, or to construct a pre-beacon attester, and
not to defer to the author. Its answer, and ours:

- **Q1 and Q5 withdrawn** ("NO BUG"; "I withdraw that attack"), conditional on one link: `b.tx.payload` (which holds
  the beacon) → `tx_id(&b.tx)`, whose implementation the brief omitted. The link is rusty-kaspa v2.1.0's version-1
  transaction id (`hashing/tx.rs`): blake3 keyed "TransactionV1Id" over the body with the payload folded in through
  "PayloadDigest" (`relay.rs` `tx_id`, `payload_digest`). It is checked twice: `tx_id_vectors` reproduces
  rusty-kaspa's own vectors, including a pair that differ only in the payload and get different ids; and on every real
  witness the host compares the guest's id with the node's before anything is proved (the `tx id ok` row of `relay`).
  What remains is the preimage resistance of blake3 and blake2b, which Kaspa's consensus rests on too.
- **Q4 re-graded "BUG"** on a new ground: the attesting sample is prover-selected (T and its ancestry), not shown to
  be the canonical DAG, so a post-beacon attacker with a private PoW branch, each superblock given a hash-only first
  parent carrying B's commitment, "can dominate the median without a majority of network hashrate". The construction
  is right and is the own-ladder case of 2.4; its conclusion does not follow, because the reading is not used as an
  estimate of the network. The relay takes `max(history, own)`: a reading can only raise the bar, never lower it, so
  a private branch that reports a low reading changes nothing. A private branch that reports a reading `k` times the
  truth must itself satisfy `attesting >= alpha x k x r x tau` with its own post-beacon superblocks, so `h >= k / 2`:
  below half the hashrate it cannot inflate at all, and above it the inflation is the least of the relay's problems.
  Mixed with the honest ladder, the private attesters are `h / (0.981 + h)` of the sample and move the median past
  one half only at h > 98%. "Representative of the network" is not what the reading has to be; "a ratchet that only
  tightens, and binds whoever moves it" is, and that is what the code enforces. Canonical DAG membership is indeed not
  checked anywhere, by design: nothing the relay reads depends on it (2.11).

No reopening. The pin proceeds on `86bb869`.

### 3.2 Documentation (README)

- D4's statement with its assumption: forging loses money while KAS trades above `ATTEST_UNIT / 125 KAS` =
  $0.016; electricity makes the real break-even lower still; the constant is immutable, the KAS price is not.
- The tip is best-effort: paid pro rata with each installment to the prover the proof names; refused tips go to the
  fee address (after F18).
- The deposit's Kaspa address is final; the page checks the checksum, the vault checks the shape.
- The reference after a silence longer than SLOW_SPAN is the newest reading; the maintenance burn is what keeps
  that from ever being the case.
- Consecutive deposits: a deposit is processed in nonce order; a blacklisted one is carried as a skip.

## 4. Design questions, decided

- **alpha stays 0.5.** Both models prefer 0.55. At the floor alpha is irrelevant (F binds until ~97 min), and the
  floor is where a sub-majority forger does best: at 45% of the hashrate his best attempt is tau ~100 min against
  452 required with a mean of 407, success 1.4% per attempt, ~$1,600 of forgone emission per attempt, prize at most
  $904 (D4). At 49%: 34% per attempt, $1,840 against $940. At 50%: one in two, $2,040 against $1,020. The forgery
  loses money at every hashrate while KAS is above $0.016; that is D4's job, and alpha's job is liveness: 0.5 lets
  honest exits settle through a 2x hashrate drop, 0.55 through 1.8x. At 40% the computed odds are 2.4e-6 per attempt;
  the stale.py runs on the 11-03 to 11-08 record gave fewer than 1 in 100,000, consistent with it.
- **ATTEST_UNIT $2 for #1, factory maximum $2.00 (F19).** The README states the KAS-price assumption.
- **Close: one holder for #1.** The close can only shrink the bridge; a coerced or stolen preimage ends deposits,
  and exits continue. M-of-N buys compromise resistance at the cost of an on-chain tally; for a $5k-to-$1M instance
  the simple shape wins. A second instance can take 2-of-2 preimages (loss of either means the bridge never closes,
  which is fine).

## 6. Lessons on the method

The targeted brief worked where the blind pass could not: F15 came from a reviewer refusing to accept a stated
relationship and asking for its proof. It also showed the brief's two weak sentences: the chronology argument
("a commitment that did not exist before B") left out the one link that makes it true, and cost a reviewer two
"critical" verdicts; and the median sentence claimed more than the code enforced. Next brief: state every binding
as a chain of hashes, and state every "needs more than half" with the set it is a half of.
