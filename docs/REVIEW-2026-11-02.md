One bundle, `~/kozmic-review.txt` on the node box: 14 files, 4,965 lines, 218,559 bytes (KaspaRelayV3, UsdcVaultV2,

models lose the file in long chats (ChatGPT's follow-up could not re-read the loops), so a precise question goes to a
fresh chat with the file attached; the first prompt mislabeled the challenge window as 1,800 blocks (it is seconds),
which one model caught.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Must fix before USDC.k

|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Count borrowing in replacement.** `relay.rs` step 2 follows the level-30 parent hashes a header lists; a private-branch T can list honest superblocks as parents and count them. `submit` ranks by `count`. Honest superblocks cannot attest the attacker's B, so the attacker mines only `requiredAttest` superblocks (100 at the floor) | our own (2026-10-20), confirmed independently by ChatGPT (fresh chat) | real. | F2 | **Checkpoint poisoning.** `finalize` sets `checkpoint = jn.t`; after one forged finalize, honest proofs cannot walk to a fake K, outbound halts for good and every later round is the attacker's alone | ChatGPT | real consequence of F1; the vault cap bounds the balance at risk at any moment, not the lifetime loss | F1 first; then decide a recovery design or state it as an exit-ending event (section 3, decision D3) |
| F3 | **Attestation priced below its cost.** `ATTEST_UNIT` is $15 of value per attester; a level-30 superblock is ~2^61 hashes, ~125 KAS of forgone reward, ~$5 at $0.04 (computed from the schedule, not measured). | F4 | **Deadline resets on every replacement.** `submit` sets `deadline = block.timestamp + window` for each heavier candidate. A griefer with a dust burn and a later-tip proof every < 30 min keeps the checkpoint still; our robots never replace a real-chain candidate; past the pruning horizon the stalled burns become unprovable from pruning nodes | surfaced while checking Gemini run 2; DeepSeek independently ("strongest finding") | real. Cost ~one proof (~$0.60) + gas + dust per 30 min; no funds move, exits stall indefinitely | one fixed deadline per round, set when the round opens; a replacement never extends it (DeepSeek: "a replacement can only shorten"); decide whether a successful challenge gets a bounded response period (D2) |

| F6 | **Tip deadlock.** The guest allows `tip <= amount`; `UsdcVaultV2.release` refuses an installment whose recipient share is 0. Self-inflicted (the burner sets the tip) but a griefing lever and a page footgun | guest `tip < amount`; payouts by cumulative entitlement: track paid-so-far, each installment pays recipient and tip pro rata of the new total, explicit dust rule (ChatGPT's follow-up) |
| F7 | **State after the external call.** `KaspaRelayV3._pay` and `UsdcVaultV2.release` update their accounting after `TOKEN.transfer` | DeepSeek | latent: FiatTokenV2_2's transfer calls no hook, but USDC is an upgradeable proxy | debit before the call, or a reentrancy guard; cheap while the contracts change anyway |

## 3. Decisions for Oz before the design is frozen

- **D1, the outbound rule:** burn-anchored freshness (4.1, lead) or fixed-depth ranking (4.2), and its parameter
  (`α` for 4.1, the depth D for 4.2). Higher resists more and delays more. Proposal in the design note with the O5
  numbers.
- **D2, round deadline:** (a) one deadline per round, replacements never extend it; or (b) the same plus a bounded
  response period when a challenge succeeds. (a) is simpler; (b) gives an honest challenger replaced near the end
  time to answer.
- **D3, after a forged finalize:** (a) no recovery: outbound ends, stated in the README as the bridge's terminal
  failure, the cap is the loss; or (b) a keyless recovery transition designed in from the start (anchored to a
  pre-forgery checkpoint, needs evidence stronger than the forgery's). (b) is research; (a) is honest and shippable.
- **D4, attestation pricing and latency:** the floor and per-dollar term after F1, for the case nobody challenges.
  Higher is safer and slower for large exits.
- **D5, outside human review:** a paid audit of the relay and the outbound guest, a public bounty above the bucket,
  or both, before January.

## 4. Design candidates from the same evening's discussion (Oz and Claude)

Nothing here is verified; all of it goes into S1 as candidates and through S2's review.

### 4.1 Lead candidate: burn-anchored freshness (Oz's idea)

Oz's line of questions led here: "what if my blocks contain hashed information an attacker can't know?", then "put
the beacon in the burn: they can't see it until a user actually sends something". A secret of ours would make the
operator a trusted party; a **public value that does not exist yet** keeps it keyless. The shape:

- The burn payload carries the Ethereum block number and hash at burn time (~40 bytes more:
  `KZB1 | target | tip | recipient | eth_block | eth_hash`). The guest already parses the payload; it puts the
  beacon in the journal.
- The relay checks the beacon against Ethereum's own history (EIP-2935, Pectra: the last 8,191 block hashes, ~27 h,
  in a system contract; confirm on the deploy fork) and requires attesting level-30 superblocks after the burn's
  acceptance of at least `max(floor, α x 9.6/min x (now - newest beacon time))`.
- A forger cannot start a private branch before the beacon block existed, and waiting does not help him: the
  requirement grows with the clock. He needs a hashrate share above `α` (about half) for as long as he takes. Burn
  size stops driving security; pricing (F3) becomes a secondary layer.
- Honest borrowing (F1) does not help: honest superblocks cannot attest a forger's beacon-anchored history.
- Every outbound proof carries at least one burn, and the beats are burns, so every proof is timestamped by the act
  it protects; no separate beacon transaction and no round bookkeeping.
- Extra beacons (one committed before the burn, one posted after it) were considered: "before" adds nothing the
  in-burn beacon does not prove; "after" is covered by the relay's own clock at submit. Kept as review questions.

Open: `α` and its margin from the variance of superblock arrival (measure on O5 and live data); honest liveness when
Kaspa's hashrate drops (the honest chain must keep meeting its own requirement; maybe a requirement that relaxes as
the window grows); multi-burn proofs (newest beacon); Ethereum proposer grinding (no help to a Kaspa forger);
prior art (binding PoW to a fresh unpredictable value is known in other settings; a reviewer may point to it, which
would help). Cost: the burn payload changes in Kasla's burn builder, the page and the robot's beat; lands in the
same guest v3 and relay V4 as the rest.

### 4.2 Fallback: fixed-depth attestation ranking (2026-10-20)

Rank candidates by superblocks attesting a fixed depth of chain below T, not by count. Kills borrowing without any
new data; does not by itself help when nobody challenges.

### 4.3 Alternative: relation (fork-point) proof (Oz: "send several proofs and prove the relations between them")

A challenge guest takes both candidates' headers, finds the split point and compares only the work after it; shared
(borrowed) superblocks cancel exactly. This is how NiPoPoW settles disputes for chains. Needs data availability: the
submitter must post its counted headers (~1,800 x ~300 B; blobs, ~4 to 5 per submit, journal commits to the blob
hashes). More moving parts; kept as the scoped fallback if the others break in review.

### 4.4 Response speed (Oz: "run the proof on 16 GPUs")

Proving parallelizes by segment (1,221 segments in the beat proof): ~16 GPUs bring the STARK from 16 to 36 min to a
few minutes plus the 72 s wrap, at about the same GPU cost; cold-pod startup is the real limit, so this wants warm
GPUs or our own multi-GPU box. Better still, a **standing proof**: while a round is open the robot keeps a fresh proof
of the honest chain, so answering a challenge is one transaction. Solves the timing tension in D2 (a late forger
leaving less time than one proof takes); does not fix the comparison rule. S6 item.

### 4.5 What no design removes (state in the README)

A majority of Kaspa's hashrate; Circle freezing the vault; forks on either chain that change headers or commitments
(each one an exit deadline and a migration, the price of no admin key); the unknown bug. A single proof can only
show work done, never that its history is the heaviest that exists, so safety rests on an honest watcher or on a
rate/price requirement; 4.1 is the attempt to make that requirement "a majority" rather than "a price".
