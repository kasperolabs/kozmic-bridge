# Review of 2026-11-16 and the response

On 2026-11-16, the day instance 2 went live, the public tree at commit `87dca0e` was given to an
independent model reviewer (ChatGPT) with the brief reproduced at the end of this file: find ways to
steal or mint, guest soundness holes, broken rescue scripts, claims the code contradicts, missing risks,
leaked secrets, license incoherence; every row anchored to a file and line. The reviewer returned 60-odd
rows and a verdict of "do not announce as trustless". This document records every row, what we checked,
and what changed, so that the next reader (human or model) starts from here rather than re-deriving it.

The short version: no change to the contracts, the guests or the covenant came out of it. The rows the
reviewer graded critical describe the private-fork forger that the design already prices, with the
conclusion inverted; the rows that were right were in the rescue scripts, the documentation and two
sentences of ours that overclaimed, and those are fixed in commit `71c69e8` and after. Users are
encouraged to point their own model at this repository; this file is written to be read alongside the
result.

## A. "The witness is self-consistent but not real Kaspa history" (rows 1.1, 1.2, the counterfeit burn, the disconnected B, the unauthenticated commitment chain; echoed in sections 2, 4 and 5)

All of these rows observe, correctly, that the outbound guest does not establish that the first-parent
headers, the commitment chain, the burn transaction or B itself were accepted by the Kaspa network. They
conclude that the attestation count therefore measures nothing. That conclusion is wrong, because the
guest never claims those things; what it proves is narrower and sufficient:

1. Every header in the PoW set passes kHeavyHash, and its **level** is computed from the hash alone. A
   level-30 superblock is about 2^61 hashes of work whatever chain it sits on and whatever its `bits`
   field says (`outbound/kaspa-prover/primitives/src/lib.rs`, `verify_header` and the level computed from `pow`).
2. A counted superblock is an **attester** when its first parent, followed by hash through at most three
   hash-verified headers, reaches a commitment on the chain `c_0 = aimr(B), c_i = H_seq(c_{i-1}, root_i)`
   (`relay.rs`, steps 1b, 1c, 2, `attest_steps`). The first-parent pointer is inside the superblock's
   PoW-covered header.
3. B is in the PoW set and `aimr(B)` is the end of the sequencing-commitment chain that proves each
   burn's acceptance (`relay.rs`, `inclusion_with`); the burn's payload carries an Ethereum block hash
   (`payload_beacon`, `pick_anchor`); the relay checks that hash against Ethereum's history and reads its
   time (`usdc/src/KaspaRelayV5.sol`, `beaconTime`, lines 260 to 271).

From these three facts: an honest superblock, mined by the network, has a first-parent hash fixed by its
PoW; a fabricated header cannot be made to carry that hash without a preimage attack on BLAKE2b. So the
attesters of a fabricated B, a fabricated burn or a fabricated commitment chain are superblocks the
attacker mined himself, each costing 2^61 hashes, each committing (through B and the burn) to an
Ethereum block hash that did not exist before that block. That is the private-fork forger. The relay
requires `attesting >= max(FLOOR, alpha x r x tau, amount / ATTEST_UNIT)` (`requiredAttest`, lines 200
to 210) and `docs/V4-DESIGN.md` prices the forger's odds under that rule (the first-passage tables that
moved FLOOR from 450 to 600 on 2026-11-15). The reviewer's remark that "the problem is not solved by
raising FLOOR" is backwards: FLOOR and the time term are exactly the price of the attack the rows
describe.

Specific rows:

- *First-parent headers are hash-only (1.1).* By design; the adversarial suite exercises it:
  `tampered_fp_header` (a rewritten first-parent header; attesting 98 to 97, because nothing points at
  it) and `rewired_first_parent` (a counted superblock's first parent re-pointed at B: "fails proof of
  work", because the pointer is inside the PoW) (`outbound/kaspa-prover/host/src/adversarial.rs`).
  Requiring PoW on chain headers would add nothing: the forger model already grants him free non-super
  blocks.
- *The beacon does not prevent pre-mining (1.2).* It does: the burn carries the beacon's hash, B commits
  to the burn, every attester commits to B. Work done before the beacon block existed cannot be made to
  commit to its hash. Choosing an old beacon is allowed and only raises `tau`, hence `required`.
- *The burn's inputs are not checked and the commitment chain is witness-supplied.* Inside a real B these
  are checked by Kaspa consensus, whose acceptance `aimr(B)` commits to; inside a fabricated B they are
  the forger's, and the forger still needs the attesters. Same model.
- *The ladder walk never asserts that it reaches B.* It does not need to: the attestation binds each
  superblock to B individually. The walk's `count` is emitted in the `Settled` event
  (`KaspaRelayV5.sol`, line 353) and used nowhere in the rule; only `attesting` is (line 348).
- *README claims GHOSTDAG blue work is verified.* True, and ours to fix: it said so from an earlier
  design. The guest does not verify blue work or GHOSTDAG ordering and the security argument does not
  rest on them. Fixed in README.md and in `outbound/kaspa-prover/README.md`, which now lists what is and
  is not checked.

## B. "The difficulty reading can be manipulated and the baseline poisoned" (two high rows)

`bits` is a free field (the code says so) and `r_bits` is read from headers. The relay's rule is built for
that: `r = max(r_hist, r_own)` (`requiredAttest`, line 203), where `r_own` is the **median** target over
the superblocks the walks expanded (`median_bits` in `relay.rs`: a forger moves it only with a majority
of the ladder, which is a majority of the hashrate over the window) and `r_hist` is the highest settled
reading of the last seven days on Ethereum's clock (`historyWork`, lines 175 to 182). A proof can raise
the reference and never lower it; `_remember` (lines 184 to 195) keeps the per-day maximum and the newest
reading, and the newest reading is consulted only when no settlement happened in seven days. The
"poisoned fresh instance" scenario needs a proof with a low median to settle first, which needs the
majority it is trying to avoid needing. Both points are in `docs/V4-DESIGN.md` 2.11.3 and 2.11.4 and in
the review of 2026-11-08.

## C. "A burn larger than the available backing is permanently haircut" (medium, plus three doc rows)

`_record` (lines 362 to 386) clamps a new debt to `outstanding - owedTotal`. In an honest instance that
quantity is at least the circulating token: every cell was minted against a deposit that raised
`outstanding`, every settled installment lowers `outstanding` by the full amount paid
(`UsdcVaultV4.release`, line 199; the fee moves to `feeOwed`, line 201), and burns below `MIN_BURN` are
never released. A valid burn cannot exceed it; the clamp is the bound that keeps a mint flaw, should one
ever exist, from draining more than the vault holds. Our RISKS line said "a burn larger than the remaining
liquidity waits", which described the bucket; it now says what each case does (`RISKS.md` 10 and 15,
`usdc/LAYOUT-V4.md`).

## E. Accepted as design notes for the next covenant generation (not this instance)

- `init` pins the authority (output 1) but not the script of output 2, the miner endowment. A stranger
  who had won the race to `init` at genesis could have redirected it. Instance 2's `init` is on chain with
  the intended drip; the exposure was the seconds between the prepare and the init transactions on
  2026-11-16. v8 pins the output.
- `advance` may pay the gap between `max_fee` and the real network fee to an output of its own. Known
  (`bridge.sil` header), bounded by `max_fee`, uneconomic against the proof's cost, and operator money.
  v8 can tighten it further or constrain the transaction shape.
- The burn builder and the robot should refuse a zero recipient, which the relay records and can never
  pay; the user's loss, not a theft, but a cheap refusal.
- `expected_current_slot` is prover-chosen; it bounds update slots from above and gives the guest no
  clock. The blacklist status is therefore "at the proven finalized state, at or after the deposit, at
  most as old as the covenant's current header". Documented; a design that wants "status now" needs a
  different rule.

## F. Considered and not applicable

- *The factory accepts any `genesisBits` and any token ids.* It is a permissionless factory; instances
  are told apart by their ids, which is what RISKS 14 says.
- *A beaconless (v2) or zero-beacon burn can be batched with an anchored one.* That is the rescue burn:
  B accepts every burn in the batch and every attester postdates B, hence the freshest beacon; the older
  burns gain nothing. `docs/SELF-RESCUE.md` describes it.
- *`extra_headers` are not given to the guest.* They are test material for the adversarial suite;
  `burnwitness/README.md` now says so.
- *PROVENANCE.md names the private commit and the export script.* Intended.
- *Deployer, robot and fee addresses are public.* They are on chain.
